# Professional Motion Design Checklist

This is the checklist for rebuilding the Flowfex video. It is based on how strong motion designers structure explanatory work.

1. One message per beat.
   Each scene communicates one idea only. No scene should try to sell, explain architecture, and show product proof at the same time.

2. Design the still frame first.
   A scene must read as a strong static composition before any animation is added.

3. Animate hierarchy, not everything.
   The first moving element is the most important. Secondary elements should support the main point, not compete with it.

4. Use anticipation, action, and settle.
   Elements prepare, move, then land with a controlled settle. Nothing should just appear.

5. Give the viewer time to read.
   Explanatory motion holds long enough for comprehension. If text cannot be read comfortably, the timing is wrong.

6. Carry motion across cuts.
   Scene transitions should inherit direction or geometry from the outgoing scene so the video feels authored, not switched.

7. Use secondary motion sparingly.
   Ambient motion is only there to support focus. If it steals attention, remove it.

8. Favor contrast in pacing.
   Build, hold, transition. Fast moments only work when they are framed by slower readable moments.

9. Apply the animation principles intentionally.
   Staging, anticipation, squash and stretch, overlap, follow-through, arcs, timing, and exaggeration should serve communication.

10. End on the payoff and hold.
   The viewer should leave on the clearest statement of what the product is and why it matters.

## How This Version Applies the Checklist

- Scene 1 explains what Flowfex is.
- Scene 2 explains the connection model.
- Scene 3 explains the repo architecture.
- Scene 4 explains the runtime orchestration path.
- Scene 5 lands on product proof and summary.

- Transitions are directional wipes and pushes only.
- Text is revealed in reading order.
- Cards and pills use controlled compression and settle, not fades.
- The graph scene is slowed down so the viewer can follow the request path.

## Apple-Style Product Film Notes

1. Restraint is part of the design.
   Fewer elements, larger type, and more negative space create confidence.

2. The product or system is the hero.
   Motion exists to frame the idea clearly, not to show off technique.

3. Camera logic matters.
   Motion should feel like a deliberate camera move or a precise mechanical reveal.

4. Holds are essential.
   The audience needs time to read and feel the point before the next transition.

5. Materials should feel premium.
   Clean edges, subtle depth, soft light, and precise alignment beat loud effects.
